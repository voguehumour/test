<Lede>
A finance team was losing three days a month to reconciliation. We didn't build an
"AI for finance" platform. We found the one task an LLM could do safely, wrapped it
in approvals, and shipped it in three weeks.
</Lede>

The brief arrived the way these always do: *"can AI do our reconciliation?"* The honest
answer is *some of it, and only if a human stays in the loop.* So the first week was not
engineering. It was sitting with the team, watching where the time actually went, and
drawing a sharp line between the parts where a model helps and the parts where it
quietly invents numbers.

## Where the model earns its place

Matching transactions to entries is pattern work — the model is good at it. *Deciding*
that a match is correct is judgment, and judgment stays with the accountant. We built the
copilot to draft, never to commit. Every entry it proposes carries its evidence, and a
human approves it in a single click.

## What shipped

A retrieval layer over the ledger, a constrained generation step that can only emit
entries in the firm's chart of accounts, and an evaluation harness the team runs every
week against a frozen set of historical months. The evals are the real deliverable — they
are how the team keeps trusting the thing after I left.

<Aside>
The hardest design problem wasn't the model. It was making *rejecting* a suggestion feel
as fast as accepting one, so the accountant never felt the tool was arguing with them.
</Aside>

By the end, the team wasn't asking whether AI could do reconciliation. They were asking
what the next boring outcome worth automating was. That's the engagement working.
